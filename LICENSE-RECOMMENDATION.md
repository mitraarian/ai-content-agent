## MIT versus Apache-2.0

MIT has been explicitly selected for the original portions within the rights held
by Mitra Arian. The earlier comparison is retained to explain that decision; it does
not create a second license option or relicense third-party material.

| Choice | Material implications |
|---|---|
| [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) | Broad reuse with copyright/permission notice retention and warranty disclaimer. No express patent-license clause. Small compliance burden. |
| [Apache-2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0) | Explicit contributor patent grant with litigation-triggered termination; preserve relevant notices, mark modified files and carry applicable NOTICE attributions. No trademark grant. Better if explicit patent terms and a multi-contributor policy are priorities. |

Neither grants patents that contributors cannot license, clears codec patents or
speaker rights, or overrides the Remotion/custom, GPL or font terms. Apache's
additional terms do not fix uncertain ownership. The project has not established
patent ownership or contributor permission, so no legal clearance is asserted.
No Apache license is applied. package.json declares MIT for the scoped original
work and remains private to prevent accidental npm publication. Preserve all
upstream notices and obtain permission for any contribution outside the rights
actually held; license selection alone does not establish those rights.
